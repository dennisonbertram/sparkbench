# ACS712 5 A current sensor module (ACS712ELCTR-05B)

## Overview
The **ACS712** (Allegro MicroSystems) is a Hall-effect current sensor. The current flows through a 1.2 mΩ copper path inside the chip, which is electrically isolated (2.4 kV RMS per the datasheet) from the sensing side. It gives an **analog voltage** proportional to the current, AC or DC, in both directions. Hobbyists buy it on the common blue/red **"ACS712 5A current sensor module"**: a 2-way screw terminal for the measured wire and a 3-pin header (VCC, OUT, GND).
- Range (x05B version): **−5 A to +5 A**
- Sensitivity: **185 mV/A** (180–190) at VCC = 5 V
- Zero-current output: **VCC × 0.5 = 2.5 V**
- Output is **ratiometric**: zero point and sensitivity scale with VCC
- Total output error ±1.5 % at 25 °C; noise about 21 mV peak-to-peak (≈ 0.11 A)
- Supply: **4.5–5.5 V**, about 10 mA

The 20 A (100 mV/A) and 30 A (66 mV/A) modules look identical; read the chip marking (…05B, …20A, …30A).

## Pinout (module, 3-pin header)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | VCC | Supply, 4.5–5.5 V (use 5 V) |
| 2 | OUT | Analog output: 2.5 V at 0 A, +185 mV per amp |
| 3 | GND | Ground |

The silkscreen on most boards reads **GND OUT VCC** from left to right with the screw terminal at the top; it is the same row, read from the other end.
The **screw terminal** (IP+ / IP−) is in series with the load. Current flowing in at IP+ and out at IP− gives a positive reading.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 4.5 V min, 5.5 V max (absolute max 8 V). **3.3 V is not enough.**
- Output: 1.575 V at −5 A to 3.425 V at +5 A (with VCC = 5 V). Output load ≥ 4.7 kΩ, ≤ 10 nF.
- Primary current: ±5 A for the specified accuracy; survives a 100 A, 100 ms pulse.
- The module isolates the sensing side, but the screw terminal and its traces are **not** rated for mains by a beginner: stay with low-voltage DC loads.

## Interface details
Conversion formula (datasheet, ratiometric):
```
I(A) = (Vout − VCC/2) / (0.185 V/A × VCC/5 V)
```
With VCC = 5 V: 2.5 V = 0 A, 2.87 V = +2 A, 2.13 V = −2 A.
For best accuracy, measure the real zero point with no current flowing (`calibrate_zero()`), since VIOUT(Q) varies a few tens of mV between parts.

## Wiring
| Module | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| VCC | 5V / VIN (USB 5 V) | VBUS (pin 40) | 5V (pin 2) |
| OUT | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC (e.g. ADS1115 A0) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |

OUT goes above 3.3 V for currents above about +4.3 A. The ADC then saturates (the reading clips), and at +5 A the pin sees 3.43 V, close to the 3.6 V absolute maximum. To read the full range safely on a 3.3 V board, add a divider: 10 kΩ from OUT to the ADC pin, 20 kΩ from the ADC pin to GND, and pass `divider=1.5` to the driver.

## Using the driver
```python
from acs712 import ACS712
import time
sensor = ACS712(26)          # GPIO of the ADC pin OUT is wired to (VCC = 5 V)
# sensor.calibrate_zero()    # optional: run once with no current flowing
while True:
    print('I=%.2fA' % sensor.current())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 64 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535. Pass `vcc=` if your 5 V rail is not 5.0 V, and `sensitivity=ACS712.SENS_20A` or `SENS_30A` for the other versions.

## Gotchas
- **Power it from 5 V,** not 3.3 V. The zero point is VCC/2, so a sagging USB 5 V rail (4.8 V) moves the zero point to 2.4 V, which reads as −0.5 A. Measure VCC and pass it, or use `calibrate_zero()`.
- **Noise:** 185 mV/A on a 12-bit ESP32 ADC is about 4 mA per step, but the sensor noise is about ±50 mA. Average many samples and don't expect milliamp precision.
- **ESP32 ADC** is nonlinear above about 3.1 V even at 11 dB attenuation. Use ADC1 pins (ADC2 stops working when Wi-Fi is on).
- **AC current:** the reading swings around 2.5 V. Sample quickly over whole cycles and compute RMS. A single read is meaningless.
- **Magnetic fields:** keep magnets and motors away from the chip, and don't run the measured wire back over the chip.
- **Wrong direction?** Swap the wires on the screw terminal (the sign flips).
