# Grove - Moisture Sensor v1.4 (resistive soil probe)

## Overview
The **Seeed Grove - Moisture Sensor v1.4 (SKU 101020008)** is a resistive soil-moisture probe. Its two gold-plated PCB forks go into the soil. It has no chip and no digital interface:
- **VCC → R1 100 Ω → fork P1**. The soil between the forks carries current to **fork P2**, which drives the base of an **NPN transistor Q2** (SOT-23).
- Q2's collector is on VCC. Its emitter is **SIG**, with **R2 100 Ω** to GND. This makes an emitter follower.
- Wetter soil conducts better, so more base current flows and **SIG rises**. Dry soil or air reads about **0 V**. SIG can never go above about **VCC − 0.7 V** (Q2's base-emitter drop), so about 2.6 V on a 3.3 V supply.
- Seeed gives no volts-to-moisture formula. It only gives typical 10-bit readings on a 5 V Arduino, where the ADC reference is VCC: **dry soil 0–300, humid soil 300–700, in water 700–950**. As fractions of VCC that is 29 % and 68 %.

## Pinout (Grove connector, pin 1 to 4)
| Pin | Name | Wire colour | Function |
|-----|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | SIG | yellow | Analog output (Q2 emitter), 0 V dry, up to about VCC − 0.7 V in water |
| 2 | NC | white | Not connected |
| 3 | VCC | red | Supply, 3.3–5 V |
| 4 | GND | black | Ground |

Source: Seeed's v1.4 Eagle schematic (J1 TWIG 2.0 mm connector: 1 = SIG, 2 = unconnected net, 3 = VCC, 4 = GND), the board's SIG/VCC/GND silkscreen, and the Seeed wiki wire table (yellow = A0, white = not connected, red = 5V, black = GND).

## Electrical limits
- Supply 3.3–5 V. Current 0–35 mA (Seeed spec). Most of it flows when the forks are in water: the emitter current is limited only by R2 100 Ω, so it is about 26 mA at 3.3 V.
- On 5 V, SIG can reach about 4.3 V. **On an ESP32 or Pico, power it from 3.3 V** so SIG stays under about 2.6 V.
- The 35 mA peak is too much to feed VCC straight from a GPIO pin. To switch it off between readings, use a transistor or load switch.

## Interface details
```
SIG rises with soil conductivity (no published formula)
fraction = V(SIG) / VCC
  < 0.29 (300/1023)  -> dry soil
  < 0.68 (700/1023)  -> humid soil
  >= 0.68            -> very wet / in water
Arduino-style value = fraction × 1023
```
Example on a 3.3 V supply: below 0.97 V is dry, 0.97–2.26 V is moist, and above 2.26 V is wet. The reading depends on the soil's salt content, how deep the forks are and how corroded they are, so calibrate thresholds in your own pot.

## Wiring
Use a Grove-to-female-jumper cable, or a Grove base shield/hat analog port.

| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIG (yellow) | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC (Grove Base Hat A0, MCP3008, ADS1115) |
| NC (white) | not connected | not connected | not connected |
| VCC (red) | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| GND (black) | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has no built-in ADC.

## Using the driver
```python
from grove_moisture_sensor import GroveMoistureSensor
import time
sensor = GroveMoistureSensor(26, vcc=3.3)   # GPIO of the ADC pin SIG is wired to
while True:
    print('M=%.2fV %s' % (sensor.volts(), sensor.level()))
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 16 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=`). `level()` returns 'dry', 'moist' or 'wet' using Seeed's ranges as fractions of `vcc`. `arduino_value()` gives the 0–1023 number from Seeed's tables. `raw()` gives one 16-bit reading for your own thresholds.

## Gotchas
- **Higher = wetter.** This is the opposite of the FC-28/YL-69 comparator modules, where AO falls as the soil gets wet.
- **Corrosion:** DC current flows through the soil all the time, so the forks slowly electrolyse and corrode. Seeed sells a capacitive version for long-term use. Switching the supply off between readings slows the corrosion.
- **Not calibrated:** readings change with soil type, fertiliser (salts) and probe depth. Calibrate dry and wet levels in your own soil.
- **Keep the top dry:** only push the forks into the soil, up to the line. Keep the connector and transistor out of the water.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).
