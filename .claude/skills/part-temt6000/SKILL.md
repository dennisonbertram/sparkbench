# TEMT6000 ambient light sensor (TEMT6000X01)

## Overview
The **TEMT6000X01** (Vishay) is an NPN phototransistor in a 1206 package whose spectral response is matched to the human eye (peak 570 nm, 440–800 nm). It has no digital interface. Hobbyists buy it on the **SparkFun Ambient Light Sensor Breakout - TEMT6000 (SEN-08688, formerly BOB-08688)** and its many clones: the collector goes to VCC and a **10 kΩ resistor** from the emitter to GND turns the light current into a voltage on SIG.
- Light current (VCE = 5 V, illuminant A): **10 µA typ at 20 lx, 50 µA typ at 100 lx** → 0.5 µA/lx
- On the breakout: **V(SIG) = I_PCE × 10 kΩ ≈ 5 mV per lux**, so lux ≈ 200 × V(SIG)
- Output saturates near VCC − VCEsat: about **640 lx at 3.3 V** (about 980 lx at 5 V). Daylight saturates it.
- Dark current 3 nA typ (30 µV on the 10 kΩ, i.e. 0 lx reads ~0 V)

## Pinout (SparkFun breakout, header pin 1 to 3)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | VCC | Collector supply, 3.3 V or 5 V (never above 6 V) |
| 2 | GND | Ground |
| 3 | SIG | Analog output, ≈ 5 mV per lux (emitter, 10 kΩ to GND) |

Source: SparkFun hardware repo schematic (JP4: 1 = VCC, 2 = GND, 3 = S) and the Fritzing breakout (SIG, GND, VCC printed top to bottom).

## Electrical limits
- VCEO 6 V absolute max (datasheet), so keep VCC at 5.5 V or less. Collector current 20 mA max, 100 mW.
- Current draw is just the photocurrent: at most VCC / 10 kΩ (0.33 mA at 3.3 V).
- Operating temperature −40 to +100 °C; the photocurrent rises about 1.18 %/K.

## Interface details
```
I_PCE(µA) = V(SIG) / 10 kΩ × 10^6
E(lx)     = I_PCE(µA) / 0.5        (datasheet typ: 50 µA at 100 lx)
          = 200 × V(SIG)
```
Examples: 0.1 V ≈ 20 lx, 0.5 V ≈ 100 lx, 1.5 V ≈ 300 lx, 3.0 V ≈ 600 lx.
The sensitivity spread is large (3.5–16 µA at 20 lx, ±60 % around typical), so the lux figure is an estimate. For accurate lux, calibrate `ua_per_lux` against a lux meter.

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| VCC | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |
| SIG | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC input (e.g. MCP3008 CH0 or ADS1115 A0) |

Power it from **3.3 V** on 3.3 V boards: from 5 V the SIG pin can rise to nearly 5 V in bright light and damage a 3.3 V ADC pin. The Raspberry Pi 4 has no built-in ADC.

## Using the driver
```python
from temt6000 import TEMT6000
import time
sensor = TEMT6000(26)          # GPIO number of the ADC pin SIG is wired to
while True:
    print('L=%.0flx' % sensor.lux())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 16 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your rail differs). `volts()` and `microamps()` give the raw quantities.

## Gotchas
- **Saturation:** with the 10 kΩ load it tops out at a few hundred lux (≈ 640 lx at 3.3 V). Outdoors it reads full scale. For brighter light, replace R1 with a smaller resistor and pass `load_ohms=`.
- **5 V supply on a 3.3 V MCU:** SIG follows the light up to almost VCC; power it from 3.3 V.
- **Not calibrated:** units vary ±60 % from the typical figure, and the reading changes about 1 %/°C. Good for "brighter/darker" and thresholds; calibrate for real lux.
- **Flicker:** mains lighting flickers at 100/120 Hz; averaging samples smooths it.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).
