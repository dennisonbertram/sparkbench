# MCP9700 analog temperature sensor (MCP9700-E/TO)

## Overview
The **MCP9700** (Microchip, "Linear Active Thermistor IC") is a low-power temperature sensor with a linear voltage output. It has no digital interface: you read the output with an ADC. Hobbyists buy the **MCP9700-E/TO** (or the more accurate **MCP9700A-E/TO**) in a 3-pin TO-92 package.
- Temperature coefficient: **10.0 mV/°C** with **500 mV at 0 °C**
- Output: **100 mV at −40 °C**, **750 mV at 25 °C**, **1.75 V at 125 °C**
- Specified **−40 °C to +125 °C** (the 150 °C high-temperature grade is not made in TO-92)
- Accuracy: MCP9700 ±2 °C typ (−4/+4 °C max, 0–70 °C); MCP9700A ±1 °C typ (±2 °C max, 0–70 °C)
- Supply: **2.3 V to 5.5 V**, only **6 µA** typical (12 µA max), so it runs straight from 3.3 V

## Pinout (TO-92, flat face towards you, legs down)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 (left) | VDD | Supply, 2.3–5.5 V |
| 2 (middle) | VOUT | Analog output, 500 mV + 10 mV/°C |
| 3 (right) | GND | Ground |

Datasheet "Package Types" (page 2) and Table 3-1: TO-92 pin 1 VDD, pin 2 VOUT, pin 3 GND.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 2.3 V min, 5.5 V max (absolute max 6.0 V).
- Output: 100 µA source/sink max (absolute max ±30 mA). VOUT stays between 0.1 V and 1.75 V, so it is safe for a 3.3 V ADC even with a 5 V supply.
- Designed to drive large capacitive loads (tested with 1000 pF).

## Interface details
Conversion formula (datasheet Equation 4-1, VOUT = TC × TA + V0°C):
```
T(°C) = (Vout(mV) − 500) / 10
```
Examples: 0.50 V = 0 °C, 0.75 V = 25 °C, 0.90 V = 40 °C, 0.40 V = −10 °C.
Put a 0.1 µF to 1 µF ceramic capacitor between VDD and GND close to the sensor (datasheet section 4.3). In noisy setups feed VDD through a 200 Ω resistor with a 1 µF capacitor.

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| VDD | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| VOUT | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC input (e.g. MCP3008 CH0 or ADS1115 A0) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has **no built-in ADC**, so you need an external ADC. On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).

## Using the driver
```python
from mcp9700 import MCP9700
import time
sensor = MCP9700(26)        # GPIO number of the ADC pin VOUT is wired to
while True:
    print('T=%.1fC' % sensor.temperature())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your 3V3 rail is different).

## Gotchas
- **Pin orientation:** reversing VDD and GND can destroy the sensor. If it gets warm, unplug it.
- **Look-alikes:** the MCP9701 uses 19.5 mV/°C and 400 mV at 0 °C and needs 3.1 V minimum; the LM35 has no offset. Check the marking on the package before using this formula.
- **Accuracy:** the plain MCP9700 is only ±2 °C typical (up to +6 °C off at the extremes). A one-point calibration at room temperature improves it to about ±0.5 °C from 0 to 70 °C.
- **Resolution:** 10 mV/°C is small. A 12-bit 3.3 V ADC gives about 0.08 °C per step, and noise is bigger. Average samples and fit the decoupling capacitor.
- **Pico ADC reference** is the 3.3 V rail: if it sags, readings shift. Pass the measured rail as `vref=`.
- **Placement:** the die sees the temperature of its legs and the PCB more than the air. Keep it away from the regulator and MCU.
