# MiCS-5524 CO / alcohol / VOC gas sensor (Adafruit MiCS5524 breakout)

## Overview
The **MiCS-5524** (SGX Sensortech, now Amphenol) is a small MEMS metal-oxide gas sensor. Its sensing resistance **Rs drops** when reducing gases are present. It reacts to many gases and **cannot tell which one** it sees. Hobbyists buy it on the **Adafruit MiCS5524 breakout (product 3199)**, which adds the heater resistor, a 10 kΩ load resistor and a heater on/off MOSFET.
- Detectable gases (datasheet): CO 1–1000 ppm, ethanol 10–500 ppm, H2 1–1000 ppm, NH3 1–500 ppm, methane >1000 ppm
- Sensing resistance in clean air **R0 = 100–1500 kΩ** (varies a lot from part to part)
- Sensitivity factor S60 = Rs(air) / Rs(60 ppm CO) = 1.2–50
- Heater: 76 mW typ, **VH ≈ 2.4 V, IH ≈ 32 mA** via the 82 Ω series resistor from 5 V (Adafruit: 25–35 mA)
- **Ao rises with gas**. It shows relative changes. It is **not a calibrated ppm meter**

## Pinout (breakout header, sensor side up, header at the bottom, left to right)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | 5V | 5 V supply (heater + sensing divider) |
| 2 | GND | Ground |
| 3 | Ao | Analog output: voltage across the 10 kΩ load resistor, rises with gas |
| 4 | En | Heater enable, active LOW. 10 kΩ pull-down on board, so it is ON when left open. Pull to 5 V to turn off |

## Electrical limits
- 5V: **4.9–5.1 V** (datasheet Vsupply). Heater voltage above the rating destroys the sensor. 3.3 V does not heat it properly.
- Current: about 32 mA (heater) plus well under 1 mA in the sensing divider.
- Ao is referenced to 5 V. In clean air it sits low (about 0.03–0.45 V for R0 = 1500–100 kΩ), but with a lot of gas Rs can fall to a few kΩ and Ao can pass **3.3 V**. For an ESP32 or Pico, put a divider on Ao (10 kΩ in series, 20 kΩ to GND gives ×2/3) and pass `divider=1.5`, or accept clipping at high concentrations.
- En is the gate of a P-MOSFET whose source is on 5 V. A **3.3 V GPIO high does not reliably turn the heater off**. Leave En unconnected (heater always on) or drive it from 5 V logic (e.g. an NPN or open-drain stage with a pull-up to 5 V, which inverts the logic).

## Interface details
Datasheet measurement circuit: Rs and the load resistor RL form a divider from the supply:
```
Vout (Ao) = Vc × RL / (Rs + RL)        RL = 10 kΩ on the Adafruit board (datasheet: RL ≥ 820 Ω)
Rs = (Vc / Vout − 1) × RL
```
Gas concentration comes from **Rs/R0** on the datasheet's log-log chart (continuous power, 25 °C, 50 % RH). R0 differs for every sensor, so **calibrate R0 in clean air** on your own sensor. The driver gives the voltage, Rs and Rs/R0. It does not invent ppm.
After switching the heater on, wait at least a second (Adafruit) before reading. For stable values, let it run for several minutes.

## Wiring
| Breakout | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5V | 5V / VIN (USB 5 V) | VBUS (pin 40) | 5V (pin 2) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |
| Ao | GPIO32–39 (ADC1) | GP26/27/28 (ADC) | External ADC (MCP3008 / ADS1115) |
| En | leave open (heater on) | leave open | leave open |

On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on). The Raspberry Pi 4 has no ADC.

## Using the driver
```python
from mics5524 import MICS5524
import time
sensor = MICS5524(32)          # Ao GPIO; rl=10000, vc=5.0 for the Adafruit board
time.sleep(60)                 # warm-up
sensor.calibrate()             # R0 = Rs now (in clean air)
while True:
    print('Ao=%.2fV Rs=%.1fk Rs/R0=%.2f' % (sensor.voltage(), sensor.resistance() / 1000, sensor.ratio()))
    time.sleep(1)
```
`voltage()` averages 32 samples (`read_uv()` on ESP32, else `read_u16()` × 3.3 V). `heater(on)` drives En if you passed `en=` (5 V logic only).

## Gotchas
- **Not a safety device.** Do not use it as a CO or gas-leak alarm. Buy a certified detector.
- **Not selective**: CO, alcohol, hydrogen, ammonia and hydrocarbons all raise Ao.
- **R0 spread is huge** (100–1500 kΩ): two sensors read very differently in the same air. Always calibrate.
- **Poisoning**: organic solvents at high concentration, silicone vapours and cigarette smoke damage the sensing layer (datasheet precautions).
- **Sensitivity drift**: continuous exposure lowers sensitivity. Adafruit suggests running it in clean air for a while to recover.
- **En polarity**: HIGH = off, LOW/open = on.
