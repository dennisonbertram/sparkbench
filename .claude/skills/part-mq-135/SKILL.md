# MQ-135 air quality gas sensor module (MQ-135)

## Overview
The **MQ-135** is a heated tin-dioxide (SnO2) gas sensor for air quality. Its resistance **Rs drops** when NH3, NOx, alcohol, benzene, smoke or CO2 is in the air. Hobbyists buy it on a **4-pin module** (common blue FC-22-style board) that adds a load resistor, an **LM393 comparator**, a sensitivity trimmer, a power LED and a DO LED.
- Detects NH3 10–300 ppm, benzene 10–1000 ppm, alcohol 10–300 ppm (datasheet). It tells you "air is worse or better than before", but it is **not a calibrated ppm or CO2 meter**
- Circuit and heater voltage: **5.0 V ±0.1 V**. Heater 33 Ω ±5 %, < 800 mW (about **150 mA**). It gets hot
- Sensing resistance Rs: 30–200 kΩ in 100 ppm NH3
- Preheat: **over 24 hours** for a new sensor (datasheet), then a few minutes after each power-on
- **AO** = voltage across the load resistor, which **rises with gas**. **DO** goes **LOW** when AO passes the trimmer threshold

## Pinout (module header, sensor facing you, pins down, left to right)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | VCC | 5 V supply (heater + comparator) |
| 2 | GND | Ground |
| 3 | DO | Digital output: HIGH (5 V via pull-up) in clean air, **LOW when gas is above the threshold** |
| 4 | AO | Analog output, 0 to about 5 V, rises with gas |

## Electrical limits
- VCC: **4.9–5.1 V** (datasheet Vc and VH 5 V ±0.1). 3.3 V under-heats the sensor and readings are meaningless.
- Current: about 150 mA from 5 V. Budget for it on USB power.
- AO and DO are **5 V signals**. AO can exceed 3.3 V in dirty air, and DO sits at 5 V through the module's pull-up. ESP32 and Pico pins are 3.3 V max: use a divider (10 kΩ in series, 20 kΩ to GND gives ×2/3) on each.
- Operating temperature −10 to 45 °C, humidity below 95 %RH.

## Interface details
Datasheet measuring circuit: Rs (sensor) and RL (load resistor) form a divider from Vc:
```
VRL (AO) = Vc × RL / (Rs + RL)
Rs = (Vc / VRL − 1) × RL
```
Gas concentration is read from **Rs/Ro** on the datasheet's log-log chart (Fig. 3), where **Ro is the sensor resistance at 100 ppm NH3 in clean air**. The chart is typical only and every sensor differs, so **calibrate Ro on your own sensor**. The driver gives the voltage, Rs and Rs/Ro. It does not invent ppm.
The datasheet recommends RL about 20 kΩ (10–47 kΩ). Most modules fit **1 kΩ (SMD marked 102)**, some 10 kΩ (103). Check yours and pass `rl=`.
DO comes from the LM393 comparing AO with the trimmer voltage: turn the trimmer until the DO LED just goes out in clean air.

## Wiring
| Module | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| VCC | 5V / VIN (USB 5 V) | VBUS (pin 40) | 5V (pin 2) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |
| DO | any GPIO, e.g. GPIO25 (through divider) | any GP, e.g. GP2 (through divider) | any GPIO (through divider) |
| AO | GPIO32–39 (ADC1, through divider) | GP26/27/28 (ADC, through divider) | External ADC (MCP3008 / ADS1115) |

On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on). The Raspberry Pi 4 has no ADC, so use DO alone or add one.

## Using the driver
```python
from mq135 import MQ135
import time
sensor = MQ135(32, 25, rl=1000, divider=1.0)  # AO GPIO, DO GPIO; divider=1.5 with a 10k/20k divider
time.sleep(120)                               # warm-up
sensor.calibrate()                            # Ro = Rs now (in your reference air)
while True:
    print('AO=%.2fV Rs=%.0f ohm Rs/Ro=%.2f gas=%s' % (sensor.voltage(), sensor.resistance(), sensor.ratio(), sensor.detected()))
    time.sleep(1)
```
`voltage()` averages 32 samples (`read_uv()` on ESP32, else `read_u16()` × 3.3 V). `detected()` is True when DO is LOW.

## Gotchas
- **DO is active LOW**: "gas" = 0, "clear" = 1.
- **"CO2 ppm" libraries are guesses**: they fit a curve to the typical chart with an assumed Ro and RL. Without calibration against a real reference the number is not CO2.
- **Hot and hungry**: the can gets hot, and 150 mA can brown out a weak USB port. It is not a 3.3 V part.
- **Warm-up drift**: readings creep for minutes after power-on and for a day or more on a new sensor.
- **Temperature and humidity** shift Rs/Ro noticeably (datasheet Fig. 4): compensate or calibrate in your conditions.
- **Not a safety device**: do not use it as your only gas alarm.
