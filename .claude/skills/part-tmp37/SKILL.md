# TMP37 analog temperature sensor (TMP37GT9Z)

## Overview
The **TMP37** (Analog Devices) is a low-voltage centigrade temperature sensor with a linear voltage output. It has no digital interface: you read the output with an ADC. Hobbyists buy the bare **TMP37GT9Z** (or F-grade TMP37FT9Z) in a 3-pin TO-92 package.
- Scale factor: **20 mV/°C** with **no offset** (0 V would mean 0 °C)
- Output: **100 mV at 5 °C**, **500 mV at 25 °C**, **2.0 V at 100 °C**
- Specified **5 °C to 100 °C** (the 100 °C end needs a supply of at least 3.0 V). It is a sensor for warm things, not for frost.
- Accuracy (G grade): ±1 °C typical, ±3 °C max at 25 °C; ±2 °C typical over the rated range (F grade ±2 °C max at 25 °C)
- Supply: **2.7 V to 5.5 V**, under **50 µA**, so it runs straight from 3.3 V

## Pinout (TO-92, flat face towards you, legs down)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 (left) | +VS | Supply, 2.7–5.5 V |
| 2 (middle) | VOUT | Analog output, 20 mV/°C |
| 3 (right) | GND | Ground |

The datasheet drawing (Figure 4, T-3) is a **bottom view**: pin 1 +VS, pin 2 VOUT, pin 3 GND (also the pin table next to Figure 24). The TO-92 version has no SHUTDOWN pin.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 2.7 V min, 5.5 V max (absolute max 7 V). Use at least 3.0 V to reach the full 100 °C (2.0 V output).
- Output: GND ≤ VOUT ≤ +VS, 50 µA load max, 250 µA short-circuit limit. Output voltage range is 0.1 V to 2.0 V, so it is safe for a 3.3 V ADC even at 5 V supply.
- Drives up to 1000 pF (typ 10 nF) without oscillating.

## Interface details
Conversion formula (datasheet Table 1 and Table 4):
```
T(°C) = Vout(mV) / 20
```
Examples: 0.10 V = 5 °C, 0.50 V = 25 °C, 1.0 V = 50 °C, 2.0 V = 100 °C.
Below about 5 °C the output cannot go much lower than 0.1 V, so readings there are not valid.
Put a 0.1 µF ceramic capacitor between +VS and GND close to the sensor (datasheet Figure 24).

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| +VS | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| VOUT | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC input (e.g. MCP3008 CH0 or ADS1115 A0) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has **no built-in ADC**, so you need an external ADC. On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).

## Using the driver
```python
from tmp37 import TMP37
import time
sensor = TMP37(26)          # GPIO number of the ADC pin VOUT is wired to
while True:
    print('T=%.1fC' % sensor.temperature())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your 3V3 rail is different).

## Gotchas
- **Pin orientation:** reversing +VS and GND makes the sensor very hot within seconds and kills it. If it is hot to the touch, unplug it.
- **Look-alikes:** the TMP36 has a 0.5 V offset and 10 mV/°C, the TMP35 / LM35 have 10 mV/°C and no offset. All look the same; read the marking. Using the TMP36 formula on a TMP37 at room temperature gives 0 °C instead of 25 °C.
- **Range:** the TMP37 is specified only from 5 °C to 100 °C. Use a TMP36 for cold measurements.
- **Pico ADC reference** is the 3.3 V rail: if it sags, readings shift. Pass the measured rail as `vref=`.
- **Placement:** the legs carry heat into the chip. Board heat, the regulator or your fingers change the reading.
