# Interlink FSR 402 force sensing resistor (FSR402)

## Overview
The **Interlink FSR 402** is a thin polymer pad (14.7 mm active circle, 56 mm tail) whose resistance **falls as you press it**: above 10 MΩ untouched, roughly 16 kΩ at 100 g, 10 kΩ at 170 g, 2.7 kΩ at 1 kg and 1.5 kΩ at 2 kg (datasheet typical curve). Force sensitivity about 0.2–20 N (20 g–2 kg). It is a plain two-wire resistor with no polarity, so you turn it into a voltage with a measuring resistor. Hobbyists buy it as **SparkFun SEN-09375 "Force Sensitive Resistor 0.5\""** (Interlink 402 with solder tabs, two pins at 0.1" pitch that plug straight into a breadboard).

## Pinout
| Pin | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 | One FSR lead: connect to 3.3 V (V+) |
| 2 | Other FSR lead: ADC input, plus a 10 kΩ measuring resistor to GND |

The two tabs are unmarked and interchangeable (the FSR is not polarised).

## Electrical limits
- No supply pin and no rated voltage; use 3.3 V so the ADC pin can never see more than 3.3 V.
- Current: V+ / (RM + RFSR), at most 0.33 mA with 3.3 V and a 10 kΩ RM. Never connect it straight across the supply without RM: pressed hard it is only ~1 kΩ.
- Do not solder slowly on the tabs (the plastic melts) and do not crease the tail.

## Interface details
Datasheet divider (FSR from V+ to VOUT, RM from VOUT to GND):
```
VOUT = RM × V+ / (RM + RFSR)
RFSR = RM × (V+ − VOUT) / VOUT
force ≈ 170 g × (10 kΩ / RFSR)^1.25      (rough fit to the typical curve, 100 g – 2 kg)
```
With RM = 10 kΩ and V+ = 3.3 V: 0.3 V ≈ 100 kΩ (light touch), 1.4 V ≈ 14 kΩ (~110 g), 2.3 V ≈ 4.2 kΩ (~500 g), 2.8 V ≈ 1.8 kΩ (~1.4 kg). A larger RM (e.g. 47 kΩ) is more sensitive to light touches; a smaller one (3 kΩ) spreads out heavy forces.

## Wiring
| FSR / resistor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pin 1 | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| Pin 2 | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 | ADS1115 A0 (the Pi has no ADC) |
| 10 kΩ from pin 2 | to GND | to GND | to GND |

## Using the driver
```python
from fsr402 import FSR402
import time
fsr = FSR402(34)                # GPIO of the ADC pin; rm=10000, vplus=3.3 by default
while True:
    r = fsr.resistance()
    if r is None:
        print('not pressed')
    else:
        print('R=%.1fk F=%dg' % (r / 1000, int(round(fsr.force_g(r) / 10)) * 10))
    time.sleep(0.5)
```
Also `volts()` and `pressed(threshold_v=0.3)`.

## Gotchas
- **Not a load cell:** ±6 % part to part, +10 % hysteresis and drift under a standing load. Use an HX711 + load cell for weighing.
- **Press evenly on the round area:** a small rubber puck or a firm flat backing makes readings far more repeatable; pressing on the edge or the tail gives odd values.
- **Untouched reads a few mV, not 0:** treat < 10 mV as not pressed.
- **ESP32:** use an ADC1 pin (GPIO32–39) with 11 dB attenuation; ADC2 stops working when Wi-Fi is on.
