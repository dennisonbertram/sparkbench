# Grove - Water Sensor (SKU 101020018)

## Overview
The **Seeed Grove - Water Sensor (SKU 101020018)** detects water drops or a leak. It is passive: there is no chip on it.
- The board is a comb of bare interleaved copper traces. One set of traces is **GND**. The other set is **SIG**, pulled up to VCC by a **1 MΩ resistor (R1)** (Seeed schematic WaterDetection, v0.9b).
- Dry: SIG sits at VCC through the 1 MΩ pull-up, so it reads **HIGH (1)**.
- Wet: water bridges the traces and pulls SIG down towards GND, so it reads **LOW (0)**. The output is **active LOW**.
- It only says wet or dry (Seeed: "LOW when water bridges the traces, HIGH otherwise"). It is not a level gauge.

## Pinout (Grove connector, pin 1 to 4)
| Pin | Name | Wire colour | Function |
|-----|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | SIG | yellow | Digital output: HIGH dry, LOW wet (1 MΩ pull-up to VCC) |
| 2 | NC | white | Not connected |
| 3 | VCC | red | Supply, 5 V (4.75–5.25 V) |
| 4 | GND | black | Ground, also the grounded trace set |

Source: Seeed schematic (connector J2, TWIG_2.0 Grove footprint: SIG, NC, VCC, GND) and the Seeed wiki wire table (yellow = D2, white = not connected, red = 5V, black = GND).

## Electrical limits
- Supply 4.75–5.0–5.25 V, current under 20 mA (Seeed wiki). In practice the board only draws VCC / 1 MΩ = 5 µA.
- Operating temperature 10–30 °C, 10–90 %RH non-condensing (Seeed wiki).
- Dry, SIG is pulled to 5 V through 1 MΩ. On a 3.3 V board the GPIO's protection diode clamps it, and the 1 MΩ resistor limits that current to about 1.5 µA, so it is harmless and reads HIGH.

## Interface details
```
SIG = 1  -> dry (only the 1 MΩ pull-up)
SIG = 0  -> water bridges the traces (wet)
```
Do not turn on the GPIO's internal pull-up or pull-down: at about 50 kΩ it is 20 times stronger than the board's 1 MΩ resistor, and a wet board might no longer read LOW. Configure SIG as a plain input.

## Wiring
Use a Grove-to-female-jumper cable, or a Grove base shield digital port (Seeed's example uses D2).

| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIG (yellow) | GPIO27 | GP15 (pin 20) | GPIO17 (pin 11) |
| NC (white) | not connected | not connected | not connected |
| VCC (red) | 5V / VIN | VBUS (pin 40) | 5 V (pin 2) |
| GND (black) | GND | GND | GND |

## Using the driver
```python
from grove_water_sensor import GroveWaterSensor
import time
leak = GroveWaterSensor(27)          # GPIO that SIG is wired to
while True:
    print('Water: %s' % ('WET' if leak.wet() else 'dry'))
    time.sleep(0.5)
```
`raw()` gives the level on SIG (0 = wet). `wet()` takes a majority vote over 5 readings, because a drop that is just touching can flicker. `irq(handler)` calls you when water arrives (falling edge).

## Gotchas
- **Active LOW:** wet reads 0, dry reads 1.
- **No internal pulls:** use `Pin.IN` with no pull, as the driver does.
- **Corrosion:** DC across wet copper slowly corrodes the traces. For long-term leak monitoring, dry it after each use or power VCC from a GPIO-switched supply only while reading.
- **Pure water:** distilled water hardly conducts and may not trigger it. Tap, rain and salt water do.
- **Not a level sensor:** it reads wet or dry. For a depth reading use an analog water level sensor.
- **Analog:** you can wire SIG to an ADC to see roughly how wet it is (lower voltage = more water), but the reading depends on water conductivity and is not calibrated.
