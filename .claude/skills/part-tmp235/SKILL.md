# TMP235 analog temperature sensor (Adafruit #4686)

## Overview
The **TMP235** (Texas Instruments) is a precision CMOS temperature sensor with a linear voltage output. It has no digital interface: you read the output with an ADC. TI makes it only in tiny SMD packages (SOT-23 and SC70; there is no TO-92 version), so hobbyists buy it on a breakout. This part models the **Adafruit TMP235 Plug-and-Play STEMMA Analog Temperature Sensor (product 4686)**, which carries the TMP235 (SC70, A4 accuracy grade), a 1 µF decoupling capacitor, a green power LED, a 3-pin JST PH (STEMMA) connector and 3 holes for a 0.1" header.
- Temperature coefficient: **10 mV/°C** with **500 mV at 0 °C**
- Output: **100 mV at −40 °C**, **750 mV at 25 °C**, **1.5 V at 100 °C**, about **2.02 V at 150 °C**
- Range **−40 °C to +150 °C**. It is exactly linear up to 100 °C. Above that the gain rises slightly (10.1 then 10.6 mV/°C), see below.
- Accuracy (A4 grade): ±1 °C typical, ±2 °C max from 0 to 70 °C, ±4 °C max from −40 to 125 °C
- Supply: **2.3 V to 5.5 V**. The sensor draws 9 µA; the board's power LED adds about 1–3 mA

## Pinout (0.1" header holes, labels on the back of the board)
| Header pin | Label | JST PH wire | Function |
|-----|------|------|----------|
| 1 | Gnd | black | Ground |
| 2 | +V | red | Supply, 2.3–5.5 V |
| 3 | Out | white | Analog output, 500 mV + 10 mV/°C |

Source: Adafruit-TMP235-PCB Eagle files (Rev A). The back silkscreen reads "Gnd +V Out"; the JST PH connector uses the same order (pin 1 GND, pin 2 VIN, pin 3 SIGNAL), matching the STEMMA colour code black / red / white.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 2.3 V min, 5.5 V max (absolute max 6 V).
- VOUT absolute range −0.3 V to VDD + 0.3 V; output current 500 µA max. The output stays below about 2.02 V even at 150 °C, so it is safe for a 3.3 V ADC with a 5 V supply.
- Drives capacitive loads up to 1000 pF.
- Turn-on time 800 µs typical.

## Interface details
Datasheet section 7.3, Equation 2: `TA = (VOUT − VOFFS) / TC + TINFL`, with Table 1:

| VOUT (mV) | TINFL (°C) | TC (mV/°C) | VOFFS (mV) | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 1500 | 0 | 10 | 500 | −40 to 100 °C |
| 1500 to 1752.5 | 100 | 10.1 | 1500 | 100 to 125 °C |
| > 1752.5 | 125 | 10.6 | 1752.5 | 125 to 150 °C |

Below 100 °C this is simply:
```
T(°C) = (Vout(mV) − 500) / 10
```
Examples: 0.50 V = 0 °C, 0.75 V = 25 °C, 1.13 V = 63 °C, 0.40 V = −10 °C.
The Sparkbench simulation covers −40 °C to 100 °C, the range where the datasheet transfer function is a single straight line.

## Wiring
| Board | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| +V | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| Out | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC input (e.g. MCP3008 CH0 or ADS1115 A0) |
| Gnd | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has **no built-in ADC**, so you need an external ADC. On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).

## Using the driver
```python
from tmp235 import TMP235
import time
sensor = TMP235(26)        # GPIO number of the ADC pin Out is wired to
while True:
    print('T=%.1fC' % sensor.temperature())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples and applies the datasheet piecewise formula (pass `piecewise=False` to use the single 10 mV/°C line everywhere). It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your 3V3 rail is different).

## Gotchas
- **Product page figures:** Adafruit's "0 V at −50 °C" is only the extrapolated line. Its formula Temp °C = 100 × V − 50 is the same as (V − 0.5) × 100 printed on the back, but the real output never goes below about 100 mV (−40 °C), so a reading near 0 V means a wiring fault, not −50 °C.
- **Power LED:** the green LED draws 1–3 mA, much more than the 9 µA sensor. Cut the SJ1 jumper on the back to disable it for battery projects.
- **Header vs connector:** the 0.1" holes and the JST PH connector are wired in parallel; use one or the other.
- **Resolution:** 10 mV/°C is small. A 12-bit 3.3 V ADC gives about 0.08 °C per step, and noise is bigger. Average samples.
- **Pico ADC reference** is the 3.3 V rail: if it sags, readings shift. Pass the measured rail as `vref=`.
- **Look-alikes:** the TMP236 uses 19.5 mV/°C and 400 mV at 0 °C. Check the chip marking.
