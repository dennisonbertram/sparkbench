# SparkFun Weather Meter wind vane

## Overview
The wind vane from the **SparkFun Weather Meter Kit (SEN-15901)**, made by Shenzhen Fine Offset. It has no electronics: inside are **eight reed switches, each in series with its own resistor**, all in parallel across the two wires. The vane's magnet closes one switch, or two neighbouring switches at once (their resistors then sit in parallel). That gives **16 directions**, each with its own resistance. Add an external resistor to make a voltage divider and read it with an ADC.

## Pinout (RJ11 cable)
In the kit, the vane's short cable plugs into the anemometer. The anemometer's long RJ11 cable carries both sensors: the **anemometer on the inner two conductors (pins 2 and 3)** and the **wind vane on the outer two (pins 1 and 4)**.

| Part pin | RJ11 | Function |
|---|---|---|
| WDIR | pin 1 (outer) | Vane resistance, to an ADC pin with a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V |
| GND | pin 4 (outer) | Other end of the resistor network, to GND |

The network is just resistors and switches, so the two wires can go either way round. Use an RJ11 breakout (e.g. SparkFun BOB-14021), or a weather carrier board (Arduino Weather Shield, MicroMod Weather Carrier) that has the jacks and the 10 kΩ pull-up.

## Electrical limits
- Passive. Use 3.3 V for the pull-up so the ADC pin never goes above 3.3 V. The datasheet's example uses 5 V, which is **not safe** on an ESP32 or Pico pin.
- Current: at most 3.3 V / (10 kΩ + 688 Ω) ≈ 0.31 mA.
- With 10 kΩ the voltages range from 0.21 V (ESE) to 3.05 V (W).

## Interface details
```
V = 3.3 V × R / (R + 10 kΩ)
R = 10 kΩ × V / (3.3 V − V)
direction = the table entry closest to R (compare log(R / Rtable))
```
Some neighbouring entries are close together: 688 / 891 / 1000 Ω (ESE / ENE / E) are only 0.21 / 0.27 / 0.30 V apart. A good ADC calibration helps there.

## Wiring
| Vane | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| WDIR (RJ11 1) | GPIO35 (ADC1) + 10 kΩ to 3V3 | GP26 / ADC0 + 10 kΩ to 3V3(OUT) | ADS1115 A0 + 10 kΩ to 3.3 V |
| GND (RJ11 4) | GND | GND | GND |

## Using the driver
```python
from wind_vane import WindVane
import time
vane = WindVane(35)               # ADC GPIO; 10 kOhm pull-up to 3.3 V
while True:
    print('DIR=%s %s' % (vane.degrees(), vane.name()))
    time.sleep(1)
```
`resistance()` gives ohms (None if open), `index()` gives 0–15, and `degrees()` / `name()` give the direction. Pass `pullup=` and `vsupply=` if your divider is different.

## Gotchas
- **Use a 3.3 V pull-up, not 5 V.** Most ESP32 / Pico ADCs take at most 3.3 V.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (GPIO32–39) with 11 dB attenuation. ADC2 doesn't work while Wi-Fi is on. Readings below about 0.15 V are non-linear, and the E / ENE / ESE positions sit near there. Average several samples, and use `read_uv()` (calibrated) where it exists.
- **The vane points into the wind** (the direction the wind comes from). Line up the small N mark on the housing with true north when you mount it.
- An open reading (near 3.3 V) means the cable is unplugged. The magnet always closes at least one switch.
- In the simulator, the vane's controls are its eight reed switches (1 = closed). Close one switch for a main direction, or two neighbours for an in-between one.
