# LM35 analog temperature sensor (LM35DZ)

## Overview
The **LM35** (Texas Instruments) is a precision centigrade temperature sensor with a linear voltage output. It has no digital interface: you read the output with an ADC. Hobbyists usually buy the **LM35DZ** in a 3-pin TO-92 package.
- Scale factor: **10.0 mV/°C**, with **0 mV at 0 °C** (no offset)
- Output: **250 mV at 25 °C**, 1.0 V at 100 °C, 1.5 V at 150 °C
- Basic circuit (just power, ground and output) works from **+2 °C to 150 °C**; LM35DZ is specified for **0 to 100 °C**
- Accuracy (LM35D): ±0.6 °C typical, ±1.5 °C max at 25 °C
- Supply: **4 V to 30 V**, about **60 µA** current, 0.08 °C self-heating in still air

## Pinout (TO-92, flat face towards you, legs down)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 (left) | +VS | Supply, 4–30 V |
| 2 (middle) | VOUT | Analog output, 10 mV/°C |
| 3 (right) | GND | Ground |

The datasheet drawing is a **bottom view** (legs towards you). Same order: +VS, VOUT, GND.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 4 V min, 30 V max (absolute max −0.2 V to 35 V). **3.3 V is not enough:** power it from 5 V.
- Output: absolute max −1 V to 6 V, 10 mA. In the basic circuit it only reaches 1.5 V (at 150 °C), so it is safe for a 3.3 V ADC even with a 5 V supply.
- Can drive only 50 pF directly.

## Interface details
Conversion formula (datasheet, basic centigrade sensor):
```
T(°C) = Vout(mV) / 10
```
Examples: 0.25 V = 25 °C, 0.37 V = 37 °C, 1.0 V = 100 °C.
Temperatures below about +2 °C need the full-range circuit (a resistor from VOUT to a negative supply, R1 = −Vs / 50 µA). The basic circuit just sits near 0 V there.
For long wires or capacitive loads, add a 75 Ω series resistor, or a 75 Ω + 1 µF R-C damper from VOUT to GND (datasheet Figures 12 and 13).

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| +VS | 5V / VIN (USB 5 V) | VBUS (pin 40) | 5V (pin 2) |
| VOUT | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC input (e.g. MCP3008 CH0 or ADS1115 A0) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has **no built-in ADC**, so you need an external ADC. On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).

## Using the driver
```python
from lm35 import LM35
import time
sensor = LM35(26)          # GPIO number of the ADC pin VOUT is wired to
while True:
    print('T=%.1fC' % sensor.temperature())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your 3V3 rail is different).

## Gotchas
- **Not a 3.3 V part:** below 4 V supply the output is wrong. Power +VS from 5 V. The output stays well under 3.3 V, so no divider is needed.
- **Pin orientation:** reversing +VS and GND makes the part hot and can destroy it.
- **Look-alikes:** the TMP36 (500 mV offset, works at 3.3 V) and LM335 (10 mV/K, a 2-terminal zener) use different formulas, so check the marking.
- **Resolution:** 10 mV/°C is small. A 12-bit 3.3 V ADC gives about 0.08 °C per step, and noise is bigger. Average samples and put a 0.1 µF capacitor across +VS and GND.
- **ESP32 ADC** is nonlinear near 0 V, so readings below about 10 °C (100 mV) are poor without calibration.
- **Placement:** the copper legs carry heat into the chip. Board heat or your fingers change the reading.
