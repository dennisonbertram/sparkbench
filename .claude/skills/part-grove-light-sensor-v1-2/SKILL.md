# Grove - Light Sensor v1.2 (LS06-S phototransistor)

## Overview
The **Seeed Grove - Light Sensor v1.2 (SKU 101020132)** is an analog light sensor. Version 1.2 replaced the old photoresistor with a **Senba LS06-S** NPN phototransistor (5 mm straw-hat, clear lens). It has no digital interface:
- The phototransistor's collector is on VCC. A **68 kΩ resistor (R1)** runs from its emitter to GND. The emitter voltage goes through an **LM358** voltage follower to **SIG**.
- Light current (LS06-S datasheet, Vdd = 5 V): **25 µA typ at 10 lux**, which is 2.5 µA/lx.
- So **V(SIG) = I × 68 kΩ ≈ 0.17 V per lux**, and lux ≈ 5.9 × V(SIG).
- The LM358 input and output only reach **VCC − 1.5 V**. The output stops rising at about **1.8 V (≈ 10 lx) on 3.3 V** and **3.5 V (≈ 20 lx) on 5 V**, so normal room light reads full scale. This is a dim-light and brighter/darker sensor, not a lux meter. Seeed's wiki says the same: the value "only reflects the approximated trend".
- Spectral response 450–1050 nm, so it also sees near-infrared.

Source: Seeed schematic for the LS06-S revision (J1 TWIG_2.0: 1 SIG, 2 NC, 3 VCC, 4 GND), the printed SIG/NC/VCC/GND labels, and the Seeed wiki wire table (yellow = A0, white = not connected, red = 5V, black = GND).

## Electrical limits
- Supply 3–5 V (Seeed spec; the LM358 needs at least 3 V). Current 0.5–3 mA.
- SIG can never go above VCC − 1.5 V, so even on 5 V it stays at or below 3.5 V. On a 3.3 V board, power it from 3.3 V anyway and it stays under 1.8 V.
- LS06-S: C-E breakdown 70 V, response 450–1050 nm, half angle 80°. Response time 20–30 ms (Seeed).

## Interface details
```
I(µA)  = V(SIG) / 68 kΩ × 10^6
E(lx)  = I(µA) / 2.5          (LS06-S typ: 25 µA at 10 lx)
       ≈ 5.9 × V(SIG)
Saturated when V(SIG) ≥ VCC − 1.5 V
```
Examples: 0.17 V ≈ 1 lx, 0.85 V ≈ 5 lx, 1.7 V ≈ 10 lx.
The 25 µA figure is a typical value measured with a specific test light. Real lamps and daylight differ, and the part also sees infrared, so treat lux as a rough estimate.

## Wiring
Use a Grove-to-female-jumper cable, or a Grove base shield/hat analog port.

| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIG (yellow) | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC (Grove Base Hat A0, MCP3008, ADS1115) |
| NC (white) | not connected | not connected | not connected |
| VCC (red) | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| GND (black) | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has no built-in ADC.

## Using the driver
```python
from grove_light_sensor import GroveLightSensor
import time
sensor = GroveLightSensor(26, vcc=3.3)   # GPIO of the ADC pin SIG is wired to
while True:
    print('L=%.1flx' % sensor.lux(), 'FULL' if sensor.saturated() else '')
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 16 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=`). `raw()` gives a single 16-bit reading for simple thresholds. `volts()` and `microamps()` give the raw quantities. `saturated()` tells you when it is too bright to measure; pass the module's supply as `vcc=`.

## Gotchas
- **Saturates early:** room light already reads full scale (≈ 1.8 V at 3.3 V). Use it for dark/dim detection, or for night-light and box-opened switches.
- **Not the old photoresistor:** older Grove Light Sensor v1.0/v1.1 boards use a GL5528 photoresistor with a 10 kΩ resistor, and GrovePi's "resistance" formula only applies to those. On v1.2 the output follows light current, not resistance.
- **Not calibrated:** use it for brighter/darker readings and thresholds. If you need real lux, calibrate `ua_per_lux` against a lux meter or use a digital lux sensor (BH1750, VEML7700).
- **Infrared:** it responds up to 1050 nm, so IR remotes and sunlight read brighter than they look.
- **Flicker:** mains lighting flickers at 100/120 Hz. Averaging samples smooths it out.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).
