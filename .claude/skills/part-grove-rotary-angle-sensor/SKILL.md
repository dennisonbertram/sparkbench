# Grove - Rotary Angle Sensor v1.2 (10 kΩ potentiometer)

## Overview
The **Seeed Grove - Rotary Angle Sensor v1.2 (SKU 101020017)** is a knob on a Grove board. Inside is a single **10 kΩ linear potentiometer (WH09-2-103)** with **300° of travel**. It is a plain voltage divider with no other parts:
- One end of the track goes to VCC, the other to GND, and the wiper goes straight to **SIG**.
- **V(SIG) = (angle / 300°) × VCC**: 0 V at one end of travel, VCC at the other, changing linearly.
- The (P) "panel mount" version is the same circuit with the Grove socket on the back.

## Pinout (Grove connector, pin 1 to 4)
| Pin | Name | Wire colour | Function |
|-----|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | SIG | yellow | Wiper output, 0 V to VCC (analog) |
| 2 | NC | white | Not connected |
| 3 | VCC | red | Top of the track, 3.3 V or 5 V |
| 4 | GND | black | Bottom of the track |

Source: Seeed Eagle schematic v1.2 (J1: 1 = wiper / SIG, 2 = unconnected, 3 = VCC, 4 = GND) and the Seeed wiki wire table (yellow = A0, white = NC, red = 5V, black = GND).

## Electrical limits
- Seeed lists 4.75–5.25 V because the board is meant for a 5 V Seeeduino, but it is a passive potentiometer and works the same on 3.3 V (Seeed's example code covers the 3V3 setting too). **On an ESP32 or Pico, power it from 3.3 V**: SIG reaches VCC at full travel.
- Current: VCC / 10 kΩ, 0.33 mA at 3.3 V (0.5 mA at 5 V).
- Source resistance at SIG: up to 2.5 kΩ (mid-travel), fine for an MCU ADC.

## Interface details
```
V(SIG)     = angle / 300° × VCC
angle(deg) = 300 × V(SIG) / VCC
```
On a 3.3 V supply: 0.55 V = 50°, 1.65 V = 150°, 2.2 V = 200°, 3.3 V = 300°.

## Wiring
Use a Grove-to-female-jumper cable, or a Grove shield/hat analog port.

| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIG (yellow) | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 | External ADC (Grove Base Hat A0, ADS1115) |
| NC (white) | not connected | not connected | not connected |
| VCC (red) | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| GND (black) | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has no built-in ADC.

## Using the driver
```python
from grove_rotary_angle import GroveRotaryAngle
import time
knob = GroveRotaryAngle(34)          # GPIO of the ADC pin SIG is wired to
while True:
    print('A=%ddeg' % round(knob.degrees()))
    time.sleep(0.5)
```
`volts()`, `fraction()`, `percent()`, `raw()` and `scaled(lo, hi)` are also available. Pass `vcc=` if the module's VCC is not 3.3 V (only if your ADC can take that voltage).

## Gotchas
- **5 V on a 3.3 V board:** at full travel SIG equals VCC. On 5 V that can damage an ESP32/Pico ADC pin. Use 3V3.
- **Wrong VCC in the formula:** the angle is relative to VCC. If you power from 5 V through a divider, or from another rail, tell the driver the real `vcc`.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (GPIO32–39); ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on. The ESP32 ADC is non-linear near 0 V and 3.3 V, so the ends read a few degrees off.
- **Noise:** carbon tracks jitter a little; the driver averages 16 samples.
- **Not an encoder:** it stops at both ends of its 300° travel and gives absolute position. For endless rotation use a rotary encoder (KY-040).
