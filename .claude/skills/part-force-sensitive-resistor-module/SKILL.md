# Force sensitive resistor module (FSR402)

## Overview
An **Interlink FSR 402** force sensing resistor (13 mm round pad, 56 mm tail) on Seeed's **Grove - Round Force Sensor (FSR402)** board (SKU 101020553). The FSR's resistance falls as you press it: above 10 MΩ untouched, about 10 kΩ at 150 g, about 2.5 kΩ at 1 kg (typical curve). The board turns that into a voltage:
- An **XC6206P332** LDO makes 3.3 V from VCC.
- The FSR runs from that 3.3 V to a **30 kΩ** measuring resistor to GND (datasheet divider, RM = 30 kΩ).
- A **TLV521** op-amp as a voltage follower buffers the divider onto **SIG**.

So SIG rises with force from about 0 V to at most 3.3 V, on 3.3 V *and* 5 V supplies. Force sensitivity 0.2–20 N. It is a touch/squeeze sensor, not a scale: ±6 % part to part, +10 % hysteresis, and the reading depends on how the force is applied.

## Pinout (Grove connector, header order)
| Pin | Name | Wire | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SIG | yellow | Analog output, 0–3.3 V, rises with force |
| 2 | NC | white | Not connected |
| 3 | VCC | red | Supply 3.3 V or 5 V |
| 4 | GND | black | Ground |

Source: Seeed's Eagle schematic (J2 pin 1 = OUT, 2 = NC, 3 = VCC, 4 = GND) and the Grove wiki wiring table. The Grove socket is 2.0 mm pitch; use a Grove-to-female-jumper cable on a breadboard.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 3.3–5 V (Seeed spec; LDO max input 6 V). At 3.3 V the LDO's dropout leaves the divider slightly below 3.3 V.
- Current: well under 0.2 mA (divider at most 3.3 V / 31 kΩ, TLV521 and LDO a few µA).
- SIG never exceeds the board's 3.3 V rail, so it is safe on ESP32 / Pico ADC pins even with VCC = 5 V.
- Do not fold the sensor tail sharply or solder on it; press only on the active round area.

## Interface details
```
V(SIG) = 3.3 V × 30 kΩ / (30 kΩ + RFSR)
RFSR   = 30 kΩ × (3.3 V / V(SIG) − 1)
force  ≈ 150 g × (10 kΩ / RFSR)^1.37     (rough fit to the datasheet's typical curve)
```
Examples: 0.83 V → 90 kΩ (just touched), 1.65 V → 30 kΩ (~35 g), 2.2 V → 15 kΩ (~85 g), 3.0 V → 3 kΩ (~780 g).

## Using the driver
```python
from fsr402 import FSR402
import time
fsr = FSR402(34)              # GPIO number of the ADC pin SIG is wired to
while True:
    v = fsr.volts()
    r = fsr.resistance(v)
    print('SIG=%.1fV R=%s' % (v, 'open' if r is None else '%dk' % round(r / 1000)))
    time.sleep(0.5)
```
Also `pressed(threshold_v=0.2)` and `force_g()` (rough grams).

## Gotchas
- **Not a load cell:** for weighing use an HX711 + load cell. FSRs drift under a standing load and have hysteresis.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (GPIO32–39); ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on. The ESP32 ADC is non-linear near 0 V, so tiny forces read noisily.
- **Untouched reads a few mV, not 0:** treat anything under ~10 mV as "not pressed".
- **Force on the pad matters:** a small rubber puck or a firm backing makes readings far more repeatable.
- Seeed's "analog output 0–650" is the Arduino Uno's 10-bit reading at 5 V reference (3.3 V ≈ 675).
