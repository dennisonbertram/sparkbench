# LM335 precision temperature sensor (LM335Z, TO-92)

## Overview
The **LM335** (Texas Instruments, also made by ST) is a temperature sensor that works like a **2-terminal zener diode**. Its breakdown voltage is proportional to **absolute temperature**, so it reads in kelvin, not °C. Hobbyists usually buy the bare **LM335Z** (or the tighter **LM335AZ**) in a 3-pin TO-92 package.
- Scale factor: **10 mV/K**, so **2.98 V at 25 °C** (298.15 K), 2.73 V at 0 °C, 3.73 V at 100 °C
- Specified **−40 °C to 100 °C** (intermittent to 125 °C)
- Operating current: **0.4 mA to 5 mA** (1 mA nominal), dynamic impedance under 1 Ω
- Uncalibrated error at 25 °C: LM335 ±2 °C typ / ±6 °C max; LM335A ±1 °C typ / ±3 °C max
- It has **no supply pin**: it needs a **bias resistor** from a supply to V+

## Pinout (TO-92, flat face towards you, legs down)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 (left) | ADJ | Calibration adjust (leave unconnected for basic use) |
| 2 (middle) | V+ | Positive terminal = the output; bias resistor to +5 V, and to the ADC |
| 3 (right) | V− | Negative terminal, to GND |

The datasheet drawing is a **bottom view** with the flat edge at the top; the left-to-right order is the same: ADJ, +, −.

## Electrical limits
- Operating (reverse) current 0.4–5 mA; absolute max 15 mA reverse, 10 mA forward.
- Below 0.4 mA the reading is wrong (it just follows the resistor).
- With a 2.2 kΩ resistor from 5 V: about 0.9 mA at 25 °C (1.2 mA at −40 °C, 0.8 mA at 50 °C).
- **V+ exceeds 3.3 V above about 57 °C** (3.30 V = 330 K). A 3.3 V microcontroller input is only safe up to about 3.6 V, so for hotter measurements use a voltage divider (e.g. 10 k / 22 k) or an external ADC.

## Interface details
Conversion formula (datasheet: 10 mV/K, proportional to absolute temperature):
```
T(K)  = Vout(mV) / 10
T(°C) = T(K) − 273.15
```
Examples: 2.732 V = 0 °C, 2.982 V = 25 °C, 3.032 V = 30 °C.
Bias resistor: R = (Vsupply − Vout) / I. From 5 V at about 1 mA: **2.2 kΩ** (2 kΩ also fine).
Calibration: because the output is proportional to absolute temperature, one-point calibration fixes the whole range. Either a 10 kΩ pot across the sensor with its wiper on ADJ (datasheet Figure 12, trim to 2.982 V at 25 °C), or in software with `calibrate()`.

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADJ | not connected | not connected | not connected |
| V+ | GPIO34 (ADC1), plus 2.2 kΩ to 5V/VIN | GP26 / ADC0, plus 2.2 kΩ to VBUS | External ADC input (e.g. ADS1115 A0), plus 2.2 kΩ to 5V |
| V− | GND | GND | GND |

The Raspberry Pi 4 has **no built-in ADC**, so you need an external ADC. On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).

## Using the driver
```python
from lm335 import LM335
import time
sensor = LM335(26)          # GPIO number of the ADC pin V+ is wired to
# sensor.calibrate(22.0)    # optional: one-point calibration at a known temperature
while True:
    print('T=%.1fC' % sensor.temperature())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your 3V3 rail is different).

## Gotchas
- **Kelvin, not Celsius:** subtract 273.15. Don't use the LM35 formula (mV / 10 = °C); that gives about 298 "°C".
- **Bias resistor is mandatory:** never wire V+ straight to 5 V or 3.3 V; with no resistor the current is not limited and the part overheats.
- **3.3 V is too low a bias supply:** at 25 °C only 0.32 V is left across the resistor, so the current is too small over most of the range. Bias from 5 V.
- **Reversed:** wired backwards it acts as a forward-biased diode (about 0.7 V). The reading is nonsense, and without a resistor the current can be high.
- **ADC range:** the output sits near the top of a 3.3 V ADC. ESP32 readings above about 3.1 V are compressed; check against a thermometer and calibrate, or use a divider.
- **Self-heating and placement:** keep the current near 1 mA; the copper legs carry board heat into the chip. Thermal time constant in still air is about 80 s.
- **Look-alikes:** the LM35 (3 pins with a supply, 10 mV/°C from 0 V) and TMP36 (500 mV offset) look the same, so check the marking.
