# DFRobot Gravity: Steam Sensor V2 (SEN0121)

## Overview
The **DFRobot Gravity: Steam Sensor V2 (SKU SEN0121)** detects steam, mist or water droplets on its gold pad of interleaved bare traces. There is no chip and no protocol: it gives one **analog voltage** on its A wire.
- Dry pad: A is about **0 V**.
- As moisture bridges the traces, A **rises** (DFRobot: "the output voltage will increase when the humidity of the sensor surface goes up").
- Range (DFRobot spec): **0–3.0 V on a 3.3 V supply**, 0–4.5 V on a 5 V supply.
- The reading is relative. DFRobot gives no volts-to-humidity formula, so pick a "wet" threshold for your own setup.

## Pinout (3-pin PH2.0 Gravity socket)
| Pin # | Name | Gravity cable | Function |
|-------|------|---------------|----------|
| 1 | A (S) | blue | Analog output, rises with moisture |
| 2 | + (VCC) | red | Supply, 3.3 V or 5 V |
| 3 | - (GND) | black | Ground |

Source: DFRobot wiki / product page SEN0121 pin definition (analog output blue, GND black, power red) and DFRobot's pin mapping comparison drawing on the product page (new version: 1 S, 2 VCC, 3 GND; old version: 1 S, 2 GND, 3 VCC). **Wire by cable colour**, since older boards swap pins 2 and 3.

## Electrical limits
- Supply: 3.3 V or 5 V (DFRobot). Use **3.3 V** with an ESP32 or Pico so A stays at or below 3.0 V.
- On 5 V, A can reach 4.5 V: too high for a 3.3 V ADC. Then add a divider on A and pass its ratio to the driver (`divider=`).
- Board size 30 × 20 × 6 mm. The connector is **not waterproof**: only the pad should see steam or water.

## Interface details
```
A ≈ 0 V            dry pad
A rises            steam / droplets on the pad
A up to ~3.0 V     very wet (3.3 V supply)
```
On the ESP32 use an ADC1 pin (GPIO32–39) with 11 dB attenuation (the driver sets it). On the Pico use GP26–GP28.

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (blue) | GPIO34 | GP26 (pin 31) | needs an external ADC (e.g. ADS1115) |
| + (red) | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| - (black) | GND | GND | GND (pin 6) |

## Using the driver
```python
from gravity_steam_sensor import GravitySteamSensor
import time
steam = GravitySteamSensor(34)       # ADC pin that A is wired to
while True:
    print('Steam: %.1f V' % steam.volts())
    time.sleep(1)
```
`volts()` averages 16 samples and returns the voltage on A. `raw()` is one 16-bit reading. `wet(threshold_v)` compares against a threshold you choose (1.0 V by default, a starting point only).

## Gotchas
- **Higher = wetter**, not a calibrated %RH. For air humidity use a real humidity sensor (SHT31, BME280).
- **Keep the connector and passives dry.** Only the flower-shaped pad is meant to get wet.
- **Corrosion:** DC across wet traces slowly wears them. Dry the pad after use, or switch the supply only while reading.
- **Old vs new boards:** DFRobot changed the socket order (old: S, GND, VCC; new: S, VCC, GND). Follow the wire colours, not the pin position.
- **5 V supply** puts up to 4.5 V on A: use 3.3 V, or a divider.
