# ALS-PT19 analog light sensor (ALS-PT19-315C/L177/TR8)

## Overview
The **ALS-PT19-315C/L177/TR8** (Everlight) is a tiny NPN phototransistor (1.7 × 0.8 mm SMD) with strong infrared rejection, so its spectral response is close to the human eye (390–700 nm, peak 630 nm). It has no digital interface. Hobbyists buy it as the **Adafruit ALS-PT19 Analog Light Sensor Breakout (product ID 2748)**: the collector goes to "+" and a **10 kΩ resistor** (R1) from the emitter to GND turns the light current into a voltage on OUT.
- Light current (VCE = 5 V, white fluorescent 6500 K): **15 µA typ at 100 lx (min 5), 150 µA typ at 1000 lx (min 50)** → 0.15 µA/lx
- Under incandescent light (CIE illuminant A) it is about 3.5× more sensitive: 520 µA typ at 1000 lx
- On the breakout: **V(OUT) = I_PH × 10 kΩ ≈ 1.5 mV per lux**, so lux ≈ 667 × V(OUT)
- Output saturates near VCC − VCE(sat) (0.4 V max): about **1900 lx at 3.3 V** (fluorescent)
- Dark current 0.1 µA max (≤ 1 mV on the 10 kΩ)
- Rise / fall time 0.11 / 0.22 ms

## Electrical limits
- Supply 2.5–5.5 V recommended, 6.0 V absolute max. Operating temperature −40 to +85 °C.
- Current draw is just the photocurrent: at most VCC / 10 kΩ (0.33 mA at 3.3 V, 0.5 mA at 5 V).
- From 5 V the output reaches about 4.6 V in bright light: too much for a 3.3 V ADC pin.

## Interface details
```
I_PH(µA) = V(OUT) / 10 kΩ × 10^6
E(lx)    = I_PH(µA) / 0.15        (datasheet typ: 15 µA at 100 lx, 150 µA at 1000 lx)
         ≈ 667 × V(OUT)
```
Examples: 0.15 V ≈ 100 lx, 0.75 V ≈ 500 lx, 1.5 V ≈ 1000 lx, 3.0 V ≈ 2000 lx.
The sensitivity spread is large (min is one third of typical) and it depends on the light source (×3.5 for incandescent), so the lux figure is an estimate. For accurate lux, calibrate `ua_per_lux` against a lux meter.

## Wiring
| Sensor | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| - (GND) | GND | GND | GND |
| OUT | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC input (e.g. MCP3008 CH0 or ADS1115 A0) |
| + (VCC) | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |

Power it from **3.3 V** on 3.3 V boards so OUT can never exceed the ADC range. The Raspberry Pi 4 has no built-in ADC.

## Using the driver
```python
from alspt19 import ALSPT19
import time
sensor = ALSPT19(26)           # GPIO number of the ADC pin OUT is wired to
while True:
    print('L=%dlx' % round(sensor.lux(), -1))  # 1 mV of ADC jitter is ~0.7 lx, so round to 10 lx
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 16 samples. It uses calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535 (pass `vref=` if your rail differs). `volts()` and `microamps()` give the raw quantities.

## Gotchas
- **Saturation:** with the 10 kΩ load it tops out around 1900 lx at 3.3 V (less under incandescent light). Direct sunlight reads full scale; replace R1 with a smaller resistor and pass `load_ohms=` for brighter scenes.
- **5 V supply on a 3.3 V MCU:** OUT follows the light up to about 4.6 V; power it from 3.3 V.
- **Light source matters:** incandescent / halogen light reads ~3.5× higher than fluorescent or white LED at the same lux.
- **Flicker:** mains lighting flickers at 100/120 Hz and the sensor is fast (0.1 ms); averaging samples smooths it.
- **ESP32:** use ADC1 pins (ADC2 does not work while Wi-Fi is on).
