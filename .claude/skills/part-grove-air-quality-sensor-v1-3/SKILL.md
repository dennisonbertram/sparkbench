# Grove - Air Quality Sensor v1.3 (Winsen MP503)

## Overview
Seeed's **Grove - Air Quality Sensor v1.3** carries a **Winsen MP503** heated metal-oxide gas sensor. Its resistance **Rs falls** when alcohol, smoke, formaldehyde, CO, acetone or thinner vapour is in the air, so the analog **SIG voltage rises**. It is **qualitative**: it tells you the air got better or worse, not a ppm value.
- Board circuit: an **SX1301 boost converter** makes 5.0 V for the MP503 heater (95 Ω, ≤ 300 mW) and an **LM358** op-amp. The sensing element runs from the **Grove VCC** to a **10 kΩ load resistor**, and the LM358 (voltage follower) buffers that onto SIG
- Works from **3.3 V or 5 V** (Seeed wiki)
- Detection range 10–1000 ppm alcohol; Rs 1–30 kΩ in 50 ppm alcohol; sensitivity Rs(air)/Rs(50 ppm alcohol) ≥ 5 (datasheet)
- First use: age it powered for **more than 48 hours** (longer after long storage), then allow a few minutes of warm-up each power-on

## Pinout (Grove connector, as printed on the board)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | SIG | Analog output (yellow wire), rises with pollution |
| 2 | NC | Not connected (white wire) |
| 3 | VCC | 3.3 V or 5 V supply (red wire) |
| 4 | GND | Ground (black wire) |

## Electrical limits
- VCC: **3.3–5.0 V**. The board boosts it to 5.0 V for the heater (datasheet VH = 5.0 V ±0.1 V), so 3.3 V is fine.
- Current: about 55 mA heater load on the 5 V rail, so roughly **60 mA from 5 V and about 100 mA from 3.3 V**. It gets warm.
- SIG: 0 V up to about the lower of VCC and ~3.5 V (LM358 output swing on its 5 V rail). On **3.3 V** SIG stays inside a 3.3 V ADC. On **5 V** it can just exceed 3.3 V in heavy pollution: power it from 3.3 V for an ESP32 / Pico, or add a divider.

## Interface details
MP503 datasheet test circuit, with the board's values:
```
V(SIG) = Vc × RL / (Rs + RL)       Vc = module VCC, RL = 10 kΩ
Rs     = RL × (Vc / V(SIG) − 1)
```
The datasheet's sensitivity curves use **Rs/R0**, where R0 is the resistance in **clean air**. They are typical only, so **calibrate R0 on your own sensor** in clean air. The driver gives voltage, Rs and Rs/R0, not ppm. Seeed's Arduino library likewise only classifies changes (fresh / low / high pollution) from the trend of the raw reading.

## Wiring
| Module | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIG | GPIO32–39 (ADC1) | GP26/27/28 (ADC) | External ADC (ADS1115 / Grove Base Hat) |
| NC | — | — | — |
| VCC | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3V3 (pin 1) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |

The Grove socket is 2.0 mm pitch; on a breadboard use a Grove-to-female-jumper cable. On the ESP32 use ADC1 pins (ADC2 stops working with Wi-Fi on).

## Using the driver
```python
from grove_aq import GroveAirQuality
import time
aq = GroveAirQuality(32, vcc=3.3)   # SIG GPIO; vcc = the voltage you feed the module
time.sleep(180)                     # warm-up
aq.calibrate()                      # R0 = Rs now, in clean air
while True:
    v = aq.volts()
    print('SIG=%.2fV Rs=%.0f ohm Rs/R0=%.2f' % (v, aq.resistance(v), aq.ratio()))
    time.sleep(1)
```

## Gotchas
- **Pass the right vcc**: the measuring circuit runs from the Grove VCC (not the boosted 5 V), so Rs is computed from it.
- **Not a ppm meter**: there is no calibrated gas formula; use it for "air is getting worse" triggers (fans, purifiers).
- **Warm-up drift**: readings creep for minutes after power-on and for a day or two on a new sensor (48 h aging).
- **Avoid silicone vapour, corrosive gases, condensation and long exposure to heavy pollution**: they permanently reduce sensitivity (datasheet cautions). Start it in relatively clean air (Seeed).
- **Temperature and humidity** shift Rs (datasheet Fig. 4).
- **Not a safety device**: do not use it as your only gas or smoke alarm.
