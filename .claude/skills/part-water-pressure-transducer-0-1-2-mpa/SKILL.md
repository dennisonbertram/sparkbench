# Water pressure transducer, G1/4", 0–1.2 MPa (0.5–4.5 V)

## Overview
A sealed stainless/carbon-steel pressure transducer with a **G1/4" (BSPP) male thread** that screws into a water pipe, pump outlet or air line. Inside, a temperature-compensated sensing element and amplifier give an **analog voltage** proportional to the gauge pressure. The modelled product is the **Seeed Studio "Water Pressure Sensor G1/4 1.2MPa" (SKU 114991178)**; the same transducer is sold under many names on Amazon/AliExpress as "DC 5V G1/4 pressure transducer 1.2 MPa / 174 psi / 12 bar".
- Range: **0 to 1.2 MPa** gauge (0–12 bar, 0–174 psi)
- Output: **0.5 V at 0 MPa to 4.5 V at 1.2 MPa** at a 5 V supply (3.33 V per MPa)
- Transfer function: **Vout = VS × (0.1 + 0.8 × P / 1.2)**, ratiometric to the supply
- Accuracy ±1.5 % FS; response time ≤ 2 ms; IP65
- Supply: **5 V ± 0.5 V**, ≤ 10 mA
- Overload 2.4 MPa, burst 3.0 MPa; media: clean, non-corrosive liquid or gas

## Pinout (three flying leads)
| Lead | Name | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| Red | VCC | Supply, 4.5–5.5 V (use 5 V) |
| Black | GND | Ground |
| Yellow | OUT | Analog output, 0.5–4.5 V |

Some clones end in a 3-pin XH2.54 plug; the colours are the same. Always go by colour, not plug position.

## Electrical limits
- Supply 4.5–5.5 V. **3.3 V is not enough** (the output would be wrong and the zero shifts).
- Do not exceed 2.4 MPa (overload); 3.0 MPa destroys it. Pressure spikes from water hammer count.
- Operating temperature −20 to +105 °C (Seeed); some clone listings say 0–85 °C.

## Interface details
```
P (MPa) = (Vout / VS − 0.1) × 1.2 / 0.8
```
With VS = 5 V: 0.5 V = 0 MPa, 1.5 V = 0.3 MPa, 2.5 V = 0.6 MPa, 4.5 V = 1.2 MPa. 1 MPa = 10 bar = 145 psi.
Some seller pages print `Vout = VCC × (0.75 × P + 0.1)`; that formula does not match the 0.5–4.5 V over 0–1.2 MPa specification (it would give 4.5 V at 1.07 MPa) and is ignored here. Below 0.5 V or above 4.5 V means a fault (broken lead, overpressure).

## Using the driver
```python
from pressure_transducer import PressureTransducer
import time
s = PressureTransducer(26, divider=101 / 68)  # 33k/68k divider, VS = 5 V
s.calibrate_zero()                            # optional: with the port open to air
while True:
    print('P=%.2fMPa (%.1f bar)' % (s.pressure_mpa(), s.pressure_bar()))
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples, using calibrated `read_uv()` where available (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 / 65535. Pass `vs=` if your 5 V rail is not 5.0 V (USB is often 4.8–5.1 V).

## Gotchas
- **Power from 5 V**, not 3.3 V, and use a divider on the output for 3.3 V boards.
- **Ratiometric output:** a sagging USB rail scales the reading; measure the rail and pass `vs=`, or read the rail with a second ADC channel.
- **Seal the thread** with PTFE tape or the supplied O-ring; do not over-tighten by the cable end.
- **Zero offset** varies a little: calibrate at atmospheric pressure (it reads gauge pressure).
- **ESP32 ADC** is nonlinear near its ends; use ADC1 pins (ADC2 is unusable with Wi-Fi).

## On a 3.3 V board
- **OUT** rises to 4.5 V, so on an ESP32 or Pico it goes to the GPIO through a divider: 33 kΩ from OUT to the pin, 68 kΩ from the pin to GND (Sparkbench adds both resistors). The pin then sees 0.67 × the output voltage (4.5 V becomes 3.03 V), so multiply ADC readings by 1.49 (the driver's `divider` argument does this).
