# INA169 analog DC current sensor (Adafruit 1164)

## Overview
The **INA169** (Texas Instruments) is a high-side current-shunt monitor. It measures the small voltage across a shunt resistor in the positive supply line of a load and turns it into an output **current** (1000 µA per volt of shunt voltage), which a load resistor turns into a voltage referenced to ground. Hobbyists buy it on the **Adafruit INA169 Analog DC Current Sensor Breakout (product 1164)**: 0.1 Ω 1 % shunt, 10 kΩ load resistor, 5-pin header and a 2-way terminal block for the measured wire.
- Output: **1 V per amp** on the stock board (Vout = I × 0.1 Ω × 1000 µA/V × 10 kΩ)
- Range: 0 to 5 A continuous (board rating), DC, **one direction only**
- Measured line (common mode): **2.7–60 V**
- Supply VCC: **2.7–60 V**, 60 µA typical (125 µA max)
- Transconductance 990–1010 µA/V; total output error ±0.5 % typ, ±2 % max at 100 mV shunt voltage

## Pinout (Adafruit 1164, 5-pin header, left to right)
| Pin | Name | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | VCC | Chip supply, 2.7–60 V (3.3 V from the microcontroller is fine) |
| 2 | GND | Ground (common with the measured supply's ground) |
| 3 | VIN- | Shunt, load side: goes to the load's + terminal |
| 4 | VIN+ | Shunt, supply side: goes to the measured supply's + terminal |
| 5 | OUT | Analog output, 1 V/A (10 kΩ to GND on board) |

The terminal block on the opposite edge carries the same VIN- / VIN+ nets; use it for currents above about 1 A.

## Electrical limits
- VCC and VIN+ 2.7–60 V (recommended operating conditions). The measured line must be **at least 2.7 V**: the chip cannot measure a supply lower than that.
- Shunt voltage full scale 100 mV typ, 500 mV max (5 A on 0.1 Ω). The shunt dissipates I²×0.1 Ω: 2.5 W at 5 A.
- Output swing: up to **VCC − 0.9 V** (typ, VCC − 1.2 V worst case) and up to **VIN − 0.5 V**, whichever is lower. With VCC = 3.3 V that is about 2.4 V, i.e. **about 2.4 A**; for more, power VCC higher (it is a current output, so OUT never rises above the clamp) but then OUT can exceed 3.3 V: keep VCC at 3.3 V for a 3.3 V ADC, or add a divider.
- OUT has a high output impedance (the 10 kΩ sets the gain): an ADC input or divider loads it. Keep extra loading ≫ 10 kΩ.

## Interface details
Transfer function (datasheet equation 3):
```
Vout = Is × Rs × 1000 µA/V × RL      →   Is (A) = Vout / (0.1 × 0.001 × 10000) = Vout / 1.0
```
0.5 V = 0.5 A, 1.2 V = 1.2 A. Replace RL (the 10 kΩ through-hole resistor) to change the gain: 20 kΩ gives 2 V/A.

## Wiring
| Module | ESP32 | Raspberry Pi Pico | Raspberry Pi 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| VCC | 3V3 | 3V3(OUT) (pin 36) | 3.3 V (pin 1) |
| GND | GND | GND | GND |
| OUT | GPIO34 (ADC1) | GP26 / ADC0 (pin 31) | External ADC (e.g. ADS1115 A0) |
| VIN+ | + of the load's supply (2.7–60 V) | same | same |
| VIN- | + of the load | same | same |

The load supply's ground must be connected to the microcontroller's GND.

## Using the driver
```python
from ina169 import INA169
import time
sensor = INA169(34)          # GPIO of the ADC pin OUT is wired to
while True:
    print('I=%.2fA' % sensor.current())
    time.sleep(1)
```
The driver averages 32 samples, using calibrated `read_uv()` where the port has it (ESP32), otherwise `read_u16()` × 3.3 V / 65535. Pass `rload=` if you changed the 10 kΩ resistor.

## Gotchas
- **High side only:** VIN+ to the supply, VIN- to the load. Swapped, it reads 0 (it cannot show negative current).
- **Reads 0 with current flowing?** The measured supply is below 2.7 V, or its ground is not joined to the board's GND.
- **Small currents:** the offset (±1 mV max referred to the shunt) is ±10 mA on 0.1 Ω; below about 20 mA the reading is mostly offset. Fit a larger shunt for small loads.
- **ESP32 ADC** is non-linear near 0 V and above about 3.1 V; use ADC1 pins (ADC2 stops working with Wi-Fi).
- It measures DC only, not mains.
